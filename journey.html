It started with where things end up.

An undergraduate survey of electronic waste in Faisalabad: who knows what it is, what people do with it, and whether they would do otherwise. 75% had not heard of e-waste. 82% did not know its hazards. 45% relied on informal recycling — and 91% said they would join a formal scheme. The question was already there: what happens to discarded chemicals, and to whoever meets them next.

Bordeaux · France
Sept 2022 — Erasmus Mundus MSc, first semester

A scholarship, and a degree that moves.

The Erasmus Mundus MSc in Marine Environment and Resources does not sit in one place. Four institutions, four countries, one thread. Bordeaux was the first.

Bilbao · Spain
2nd semester · research internship, Jan – Jun 2023

The first embryos.

At the UPV/EHU PIE Marine Station: preparing water accommodated fractions from heavy and light oils, running sea urchin embryo toxicity tests, and quantifying the chemicals by LC-HRMS. Fertilisation success fell. Larval development faltered. This is where the work moved from surveying chemicals to measuring what they do to something developing.

Liège · Belgium
3rd semester

Third country, same thread.

The last taught semester of the MER programme before the thesis — and before the move to Germany that turned the thread into a specialism.

Leipzig · Germany
Helmholtz Centre for Environmental Research (UFZ) · 2024 – present

Where the question got specific.

The master thesis — a high-content zebrafish assay comparing BPA with four of its substitutes — became a paper in Aquatic Toxicology. The work continued into three European projects: PARC, PrecisionTox, and DiPEM. From “where do chemicals go” to “which endpoint, at which concentration, by which mechanism”.
