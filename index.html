About Me

I am a doctoral researcher in NeXED DC4 at the Institute of Environmental Medicine, Karolinska Institutet, Sweden. My research focuses on mechanism-based assessment of endocrine disruptors for human health.

I explore zebrafish mechanistic evidence, including transcriptomics, within adverse outcome pathways. This work combines experimental and computational approaches with literature-based analysis and considers cross-species extrapolation and regulatory application.

My PhD research

Four connected studies, from published evidence to responses across life stages.

Study 01 / 04 · Literature evidence

Exploring AI-assisted screening and data extraction from toxicology literature: the case of bisphenols

Research question

How can AI assist the identification and extraction of relevant evidence from bisphenol toxicology studies?

This study explores AI-assisted literature screening and structured data extraction. The focus is on how extracted evidence can be checked against the source literature and organised for toxicological assessment.

Literature screening · Data extraction · Evidence evaluation

Study 02 / 04 · Mechanistic framework

Adverse outcome networks as a framework to assess endocrine disruptors: key events and bisphenol classification

Research question

How can key events within adverse outcome networks support the assessment and classification of bisphenols?

This study examines how mechanistic evidence can be organised into connected key events and adverse outcomes. It explores the role of these relationships in comparing bisphenols and supporting endocrine disruptor assessment.

Adverse outcome networks · Key events · Bisphenol classification

Study 03 / 04 · Embryo responses

Transcriptomic assessment of bisphenols in the zebrafish embryo toxicity assay

Research question

What can transcriptomic responses tell us about the mechanisms associated with bisphenol exposure in zebrafish embryos?

This study focuses on gene-expression responses to bisphenols in the zebrafish embryo model. It explores how molecular responses can inform mechanistic interpretation and connect with the broader adverse outcome network.

Transcriptomics · Zebrafish embryos · Mechanistic interpretation

Study 04 / 04 · Adult responses

Long-term effects of bisphenols in the adult zebrafish assay

Research question

How can studies in adult zebrafish extend the assessment of bisphenol effects beyond the embryo stage?

This study examines long-term effects of bisphenols in the adult zebrafish model. Its focus complements the embryo work by considering effects at a later life stage and their relevance to endocrine disruptor assessment.

Adult zebrafish · Long-term effects · Endocrine assessment

Zebrafish Assays

Experimental screening to connect chemical exposure with organism-level developmental and functional endpoints.

Development

Hatching, malformations, edema, growth, and survival endpoints.

Cardiotoxicity

Heart rate and visible cardiac phenotypes during early development.

Neuroactivity

Behavioral response patterns and potential neuroactive signatures.

Interpretation

Phenotype profiles linked to toxic ratios and mechanism-of-action hypotheses.

Toxicity Models

I use baseline toxicity prediction and toxic-ratio analysis to distinguish expected narcotic toxicity from potential specific modes of action.

Model-to-assay logic

Predicted baseline toxicity → observed zebrafish effect concentration → toxic ratio → candidate MOA grouping and prioritization.
